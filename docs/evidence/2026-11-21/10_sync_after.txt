- All three start right after a multiple of wal2delta.checkpoint_interval (200). Seven other multiples passed.
- Two of three end exactly when a predictive-optimization OPTIMIZE that read the stall's last version commits.
- Stall 3 happened at 500 Hz with ~0.4 MB/s of WAL and a near-empty slot, so WAL volume does not trigger a stall.

## Conclusion
The stall is inside the managed Lakehouse Sync writer (wal2delta) and is NOT caused by the per-row trigger WAL
volume. That volume (820 B/row, ~1.4 MB/s at 1,750 rows/s) decided how fast WAL piled up behind a stalled slot
(2.2 GB in 1.5 h on 2026-11-20). It also added ~0.7 ms of Postgres time per row, which together with the
consumer's 3-partition skew made the primary the bottleneck. After the fix the WAL rate per row is lower and
the primary has headroom. The stall itself is escalated with this timeline; the health check now flags it
within 2-10 minutes instead of an hour (lakets_sync_slot).
